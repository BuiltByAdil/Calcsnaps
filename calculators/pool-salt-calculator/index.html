If you've just switched to a saltwater pool — or your salt cell is throwing a "low salt" or "high salt" fault — getting the ppm right is the single most important number in your entire system. Too low, and your chlorine generator can't produce chlorine (or worse, it can damage the cell trying). Too high, and you risk corroding metal fittings and tripping safety cutoffs.

This pool salt calculator handles all three situations in one place: adding salt, lowering salt that's too high, and — unlike most calculators — figuring out your pool's volume if you don't already know it, with the result automatically usable in the other two tabs.

1. What Is Pool Salt PPM and Why It Matters

PPM stands for "parts per million" — essentially milligrams of salt per liter of water. It's the standard unit salt chlorine generators use to measure salinity. Your salt cell uses electrolysis to convert dissolved salt (sodium chloride) into chlorine, which sanitizes the water and then converts back into salt in a continuous cycle. This is why salt isn't "used up" the way liquid or tablet chlorine is — you only lose it through splash-out, backwashing, and dilution from rain or top-off water.

Most manufacturers recommend a range of 2,700–3,400 ppm, with 3,200 ppm being the most commonly cited optimal target — though this varies by brand (see the chart in Section 3).

2. The Pool Salt Formula, Explained

Salt to Add (lbs) = Gallons × 8.35 × (Target PPM − Current PPM) ÷ 1,000,000

The constant 8.35 is the weight of one gallon of water in pounds. Multiplying it by the ppm difference (as a fraction of a million) gives you the weight of salt required to shift that concentration across your entire pool volume.

Worked example: A 20,000-gallon pool currently reads 1,000 ppm and you want to reach 3,200 ppm.

20,000 × 8.35 × (3,200 − 1,000) ÷ 1,000,000 = 20,000 × 8.35 × 2,200 ÷ 1,000,000 = 367 lbs of salt (about 9-10 bags of 40 lb pool salt).

As a rule of thumb: one standard 40 lb bag raises 10,000 gallons by roughly 480 ppm.

5. How to Add Salt to Your Pool Correctly

  1. Test first. Use a digital salt meter or test strips to confirm your current ppm before adding anything.
  2. Use pool-grade salt only. Never table salt — see Section 8 for why.
  3. Turn the pump on before you start, and keep it running.
  4. Broadcast the salt slowly around the pool's perimeter in the deep end — never dump it in one spot, and never pour it through the skimmer.
  5. Brush the floor where salt settles to help it dissolve and avoid staining the pool surface.
  6. Wait 24 hours with the pump running, then retest before turning the salt cell back to full output.

6. Salt Level Too High? How to Lower It

Unlike calcium or pH, salt cannot be filtered, boiled off, or chemically neutralized out of pool water — the sodium chloride stays dissolved. The only way to reduce ppm is to replace some pool water with fresh (low-salt) water.

Percent to drain = 1 − (Target PPM ÷ Current PPM)

Example: lowering from 4,000 ppm to 3,200 ppm → 1 − (3,200 ÷ 4,000) = 0.20, or 20% of the pool needs to be drained and refilled with fresh water.

Use the Lower Salt tab above to get the exact percentage and gallons for your pool — and drain gradually, retesting as you go, since it's easy to overshoot.

7. Don't Know Your Pool's Volume? Here's How to Find It

Every salt calculation depends on an accurate gallon count, so if you're not sure of your pool's volume, use the industry-standard shape formulas below (all measurements in feet):

  • Rectangular: Length × Width × Average Depth × 7.5
  • Round: Diameter² × Average Depth × 5.9
  • Oval: Length × Width × Average Depth × 6.7

The Pool Volume tab above calculates this for you and lets you send the result directly into the Add Salt or Lower Salt tab with one click — no need to re-enter numbers or use a separate tool.

8. Common Mistakes That Ruin Salt Cells

  • Using table salt: it contains anti-caking agents and iodine that cloud water and can damage the cell — always use pool-grade salt (99%+ pure sodium chloride, no additives).
  • Ignoring the manufacturer's exact range: "close to 3,200" isn't the same as your specific brand's spec — see Section 3.
  • Adding all the salt at once without testing first: if your meter reading was off, you can overshoot into fault-code territory.
  • Draining too much water at once to fix high salt — go gradually and retest.
  • Forgetting that rain and top-off water dilute salt over time — recheck ppm monthly, not just once a season.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

How much salt do I add to my pool?

Use the formula: Salt to add (lbs) = Pool gallons × 8.35 × (target ppm − current ppm) ÷ 1,000,000. For example, a 20,000-gallon pool going from 1,000 ppm to 3,200 ppm needs about 367 lbs of salt, or roughly 9-10 standard 40 lb bags.

What is the ideal salt level for a saltwater pool?

Most salt chlorine generators run best between 2,700 and 3,400 ppm, with 3,200 ppm commonly cited as optimal. The exact ideal range depends on your salt cell manufacturer — Hayward and Pentair typically recommend 3,200-3,400 ppm — so always check your equipment's manual.

How many 40 lb bags of salt do I need?

One standard 40 lb bag raises approximately 10,000 gallons by about 480 ppm. Divide your total pounds of salt needed by 40 and round up to the nearest whole bag.

Can I use regular table salt in my pool?

No. Table salt contains anti-caking agents, iodine, and other additives that can cloud your water or damage your salt chlorine generator. Always use pool-grade salt, which is purer, additive-free sodium chloride.

How do I lower the salt level in my pool if it's too high?

Salt can't be filtered or chemically removed. The only fix is draining a portion of the pool and refilling with fresh water. The percentage to drain equals 1 minus (target ppm ÷ current ppm) — lowering from 4,000 to 3,200 ppm requires draining about 20% of the pool.

How long does pool salt take to dissolve?

Pool salt typically dissolves within 24 hours with the pump running continuously. Brushing the pool floor after adding salt speeds up dissolving and prevents staining.

This calculator provides estimates for general guidance only. Salt cell tolerances vary by manufacturer and model — always confirm your target range against your equipment's official manual before dosing.
