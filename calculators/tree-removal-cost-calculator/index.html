How Much Does Tree Removal Cost?

Most homeowners pay roughly $400 to $1,200 to remove one tree, and published national averages cluster around $700–$900. Small trees under 25 feet can run $150 to $500, while very large trees over 80 feet can reach $3,500 or more. The cost of tree removal depends mainly on height, trunk diameter, access and tree condition.

Storm Damage Tree Removal Cost

Storm damage tree removal commonly costs $500 to $5,000+, depending on where the tree landed and how urgent the job is. A tree lying in an open yard sits at the low end. A tree on a roof, fence or power line, or a job done as emergency service, pushes toward the high end, since emergency work often adds 50–100%.

Example with this calculator: a 60 ft, 24 in medium-hardwood tree that is storm-damaged, next to a house, with emergency service comes to about $2,150–$3,040.

If several trees came down, use the Storm Cleanup (Multi-Tree) tab to price them together. Storm-related removal is often the case where insurance helps, see the coverage section below.

Cost to Cut Down a Tree by Size and Type

Cost to cut down a 30-foot tree

A 30-foot tree sits between small and medium, typically about $350–$700 in an open yard with easy access. Close proximity to a house or power lines pushes it higher.

Large tree removal cost

Trees over 60 feet need heavier rigging and often a bucket truck or crane. Expect roughly $1,000–$2,200 for 60–80 ft trees and $1,800–$3,500+ above 80 ft.

Palm tree removal cost

Some published guides treat palms as lighter to cut than dense hardwoods, so the calculator uses 0.9x as a planning assumption. Tall palms near structures still need careful rigging, so real quotes can differ.

Emergency tree removal cost

Emergency or after-hours removal commonly adds 50–100%. The calculator applies +50%, the low end, so compare it with urgent quotes.

Crane and bucket-truck cost

Very tall trees, tight access and work over structures often need a bucket truck or crane, and some published guides note the price jumps around 60 ft for this reason. The calculator adds a flat $550 when you tick it and warns you above 100 ft, where the estimate is extrapolated.

Stump Grinding and Debris Removal Cost

Most quotes do not include stump grinding or haul-away automatically.

  • Stump grinding: commonly $3–$7 per inch of diameter with a $100–$150 minimum, roughly $100–$500 per stump. The calculator uses $5 per inch.
  • Debris haul-away: often $75–$150, depending on the volume of wood and branches.
  • Permit help: where required, commonly $60–$150.

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Tree Removal?

It depends on why the tree came down. As a general pattern (confirm with your policy):

  • Storm, wind or lightning damage to a covered structure is commonly covered.
  • A tree that falls in the yard without touching a structure often gets only limited, capped debris-removal coverage.
  • Disease, rot or routine removal is typically not covered, since insurance covers sudden accidental damage.
  • A neighbor's tree that damages your structure may be handled under your own policy, depending on the circumstances and policy terms. If it lands in your yard with no damage, expect the same limited debris coverage.
Important: This is general education based on common policy patterns, not a review of your policy. Coverage varies by insurer, state and policy language, so confirm with your provider first.

How to Save on Tree Removal

  • Get multiple written quotes. Pricing for the same job can vary a lot.
  • Ask about off-season timing. Demand is often lower in late fall and winter, outside storm season.
  • Bundle trees and stump work into one visit and ask about a multi-tree discount.
  • Offer to keep the wood. Some arborists discount the job if they don't have to haul it.
  • Verify insurance and credentials. A quote far below the others can signal an uninsured operator. You can check ISA Certified Arborist status through the International Society of Arboriculture.
