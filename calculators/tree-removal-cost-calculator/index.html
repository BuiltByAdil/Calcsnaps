Tree Removal Cost Calculator: Get an Instant, Transparent Estimate

Tree removal pricing varies enormously — from a couple hundred dollars for a small tree in an open yard to several thousand for a large, hazardous tree tangled near power lines. Most cost guides give you a single number without explaining where it comes from, which makes it hard to sanity-check a contractor's quote.

What Drives Tree Removal Cost

Tree removal pricing is built from several factors stacked together, not just one number. This calculator uses the same categories professional estimators typically consider:

  • Height: The single biggest driver — taller trees need more climbing, rigging, and drop-zone control.
  • Trunk diameter: Thicker trunks take longer to cut and require more powerful equipment.
  • Species: Dense hardwoods (oak, hickory) are heavier and harder to cut than softwoods (pine, birch).
  • Access: A tree behind a fence, on a slope, or with no truck access takes longer and may need extra equipment.
  • Proximity to structures or power lines: Working near a house or power line requires more careful rigging and sometimes utility coordination, which raises cost.
  • Condition: Dead, hazardous, or storm-damaged trees carry higher risk and are priced accordingly.
  • Regional labor costs: Coastal and high-cost-of-living regions generally run higher than the national average.
Note on methodology: This calculator's base costs and adjustment multipliers (e.g. difficult access ×1.5, near power lines ×1.4) are derived from commonly-cited ranges aggregated across multiple public tree-service pricing guides, not a single official source or proprietary invoice database. They represent typical relative cost impact — e.g. "difficult access roughly doubles the base cost range" — rather than precise, independently-validated coefficients. Treat every online tree removal calculator (including this one) as a starting-point range for comparing quotes, not a guaranteed price.

Stump Grinding & Debris Removal

Most tree removal quotes do not automatically include stump grinding or debris haul-away — these are typically priced as separate line items.

  • Stump grinding: Usually priced per inch of stump diameter, commonly around $2–$3 per inch — a small stump might run $75, a large one $300–$400+.
  • Debris haul-away: Often a flat fee of roughly $75–$150 depending on the volume of wood and branches.
  • Permits: Where required, permit assistance or fees commonly add $50–$150.

Insurance Coverage Basics

Whether tree removal is covered by homeowners insurance depends heavily on why the tree needs to come down — not just that it does. As a general pattern (always confirm with your specific policy):

  • Storm, wind, or lightning damage to a covered structure is commonly covered under most standard homeowner policies.
  • A tree that falls in the yard without touching a structure often has limited, capped debris-removal coverage rather than full coverage.
  • Disease, rot, or routine/aesthetic removal is typically not covered, since insurance covers sudden accidental damage, not gradual deterioration or maintenance.
  • A neighbor's tree falling on your property is commonly covered under your own policy, regardless of whose tree it originally was.

Use the Insurance Helper tab above for a quick, situation-specific read on this — then call your insurer to confirm before you pay out of pocket.

Important: This tool provides general educational information based on common homeowner policy patterns, not a review of your actual policy. Coverage varies by insurer, state, and specific policy language — always confirm directly with your insurance provider before assuming coverage either way.

How to Save on Tree Removal

  • Get multiple quotes — pricing for the same job can vary significantly between contractors.
  • Ask about off-season timing — many companies see lower demand in late fall/winter outside of storm season.
  • Bundle multiple trees — if you have several trees to remove, ask about a multi-tree discount for a single visit.
  • Keep the wood — some arborists will discount the job if you keep the cut wood for firewood, saving them hauling costs.
  • Verify insurance and licensing — a quote significantly below others can indicate an uninsured or unlicensed operator, which carries real liability risk for you as the property owner. You can check whether a contractor holds a current ISA Certified Arborist credential through the International Society of Arboriculture's certification search tool.
