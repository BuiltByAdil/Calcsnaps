If you're comparing UCAT calculators, the single most important thing to check is whether the tool has actually been updated for the 2025 format change — a lot of older resources online are still built around a test that no longer exists. This calculator uses the current three-subtest, 900-2700 format and the UCAT Consortium's own published 2025 test statistics, not outdated pre-2025 numbers dressed up as current. 2026 test statistics aren't published yet — until they are, 2025's are the most recent official dataset, and that's what this calculator uses throughout.

1. What Changed in 2025

From 2025, the UCAT Consortium withdrew the Abstract Reasoning subtest, stating it had lower predictive validity than the other sections. That leaves three cognitive subtests — Verbal Reasoning, Decision Making, and Quantitative Reasoning — plus the Situational Judgement Test (SJT), which was always scored separately anyway.

Cognitive subtests4 (VR, DM, QR, AR)3 (VR, DM, QR)

This means scores from 2024 and earlier are not directly comparable to 2025+ scores without a proper statistical conversion — a flat proportional scaling (like multiplying an old score by 0.75) tends to run noticeably high, because score distributions aren't uniform across the range. If you need to compare an old score to the current scale, treat any simple multiplication-based conversion with caution and look for one built on the UCAT Consortium's own re-scored historical data.

2. How UCAT Scoring Actually Works

Each cognitive subtest (VR, DM, QR) is marked on the number of correct answers, with no penalty for wrong answers — so it's always worth attempting every question. Decision Making includes some multi-response questions worth 2 marks, with partial credit available. Your raw marks are then converted to a scaled score between 300 and 900 for each section using a statistical process the Consortium doesn't publish in exact table form — this is why practice-test "raw to scaled" calculators, including the one on this page, can only ever give an estimate.

Total Score = VR (300-900) + DM (300-900) + QR (300-900)

Giving a possible range of 900 to 2700. The SJT is reported separately as a Band (1-4) and is not added to this total.

3. Deciles vs Percentiles

A decile splits the entire cohort into ten equal-sized groups, ranked by score. UCAT Consortium data publishes nine boundary scores — the 1st decile boundary sits at the 10th percentile, the 9th decile boundary sits at the 90th percentile — and those nine boundaries mark the edges between ten numbered bands. So a score at or above the 9th decile boundary (2220) falls in decile band 10 — the top 10% of the cohort — which is standard decile numbering, not an error: "decile boundary" (the cutoff score) and "decile band" (which of the ten groups you're in) are two different things that happen to share the word "decile."

A percentile is more granular — it tells you the exact proportion of candidates you outscored. This calculator estimates your percentile by interpolating between the official published decile boundaries, since UCAT doesn't publish every single percentile value. Below the 1st decile boundary (1580), the published data only tells us a score is "below the 10th percentile" — not precisely which percentile — so this calculator reports "<10th" rather than inventing false precision like "6th percentile."

4. Worked Example

Using the calculator's defaults: VR 610, DM 650, QR 680.

Total = 610 + 650 + 680 = 1940. Against the official 2025 boundaries (…1880 at the 5th decile, 1950 at the 6th…), 1940 sits just inside the 6th decile band — meaning this score beats roughly the middle of the cohort, modestly above the 2025 mean of 1891.

5. The Situational Judgement Test

The SJT presents scenarios relevant to healthcare professionalism and asks you to judge how appropriate or important different responses are. Your answers are compared to a panel of experts' model responses, with partial credit for close answers, and the result is reported as a Band:

  • Band 1 — a high degree of concordance with the expert panel.
  • Band 2 — good concordance.
  • Band 3 — modest concordance.
  • Band 4 — low concordance; some universities flag this band for closer review.

Universities use SJT bands differently — some set a minimum band (commonly Band 3 or better) as an interview requirement rather than ranking candidates by band, so check each university's specific published policy rather than assuming a universal rule.

6. What Counts as a Good Score

There's no official pass mark. As a general, non-university-specific reference using the official 2025 data: scores above roughly 2100 (8th decile, top 20%) are generally considered strong, and above roughly 2220 (9th decile, top 10%) are excellent by most measures. But the honest answer is that "good" depends entirely on where you're applying — some universities use UCAT as a hard cut-off, others weight it alongside GCSEs, predicted grades, and the personal statement, and thresholds shift year to year based on that year's applicant pool. Always check each university's current published entry requirements directly.

8. Methodology & Limitations

  • Decile boundaries, subtest means, and the overall mean are taken from the UCAT Consortium's final 2025 UK test statistics (41,354 candidates, data valid to 26 September 2025) — the most recent complete official dataset. 2026 statistics are not yet published; this page will be updated once they are.
  • The 95th (2310) and 99th (2460) percentile anchor points above the 9th decile boundary are drawn from the same 2025 UCAT Consortium dataset, as reported alongside the decile table by UCAT preparation providers citing the Consortium's figures.
  • Percentile estimates are interpolated between the nine published decile boundaries (plus the published 95th and 99th percentile anchor points above the 9th decile) — they are a reasonable estimate, not an official Consortium percentile lookup.
  • The raw-mark estimator uses simple proportional scaling across each section's raw mark range, mapped onto the 300-900 scaled range. UCAT does not publish an exact raw-to-scaled conversion table, so this is a rough guide for practice tests only — your real UCAT scaled score depends on the specific test form's statistical difficulty adjustment.
  • This tool does not predict admission outcomes. University decisions weigh UCAT alongside academic grades, personal statements, and interviews, using each institution's own (and frequently changing) policy.
  • Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sourced directly from the UCAT Consortium or Pearson. Always treat your official Pearson score report as authoritative.
