Negative Marking Calculator: Calculate Your Exact Score for NEET, JEE, UPSC & SSC

Negative marking is used across almost every major Indian competitive exam — NEET, JEE, UPSC Civil Services, SSC CGL, banking exams, and CAT — specifically to discourage random guessing. Every wrong answer costs you more than just the marks you missed out on: it costs a penalty on top. That makes accuracy, not just knowledge, one of the biggest factors in your final score.

This calculator does three things most negative-marking tools don't combine in one place: it calculates your subject-wise score (since exams like NEET have different sections with the same overall pattern), it works out your break-even guessing probability (the exact confidence level where guessing stops being a loss), and it lets you compare two attempt strategies side by side.

The Negative Marking Formula

Every negative marking system, regardless of exam, follows the same underlying structure:

Final Score = (Correct × Marks per Question) − (Wrong × Marks per Question × Negative Ratio)

Unattempted questions are excluded entirely from both sides of this equation in every major Indian competitive exam — leaving a question blank never reduces your score, it simply doesn't add to it either.

Worked Example (NEET-style, +4/−1)

Suppose you answer 150 questions: 120 correct, 30 wrong, out of a total 180 questions.

  • Marks gained: 120 × 4 = 480
  • Marks deducted: 30 × 1 = 30
  • Final score: 480 − 30 = 450

What Is Break-Even Probability?

This is the single most useful piece of exam-strategy math that most students never calculate. Break-even probability is the minimum chance of being correct at which attempting a question stops being a net loss on average. Whether blind guessing (no elimination at all) clears that bar depends on both the negative ratio and the number of answer options — it is not the same answer for every exam, which is an important nuance most guides skip.

Break-Even Probability = Negative Ratio ÷ (1 + Negative Ratio)
Example (1/4 ratio, NEET/JEE): 0.25 ÷ 1.25 = 0.20 → you need at least a 20% chance of being correct before attempting is worth it on average.

Here's the nuance: with 4 answer options and a 1/4 ratio, a completely blind random guess already has a 25% chance of being correct — which is above the 20% break-even threshold. So for this specific, very common combination, even fully random guessing has a slightly positive expected value on average. That changes as soon as either number shifts: at a 1/3 ratio (like UPSC Prelims), the break-even threshold rises to 25% — exactly equal to a blind 4-option guess — so blind guessing there is a wash at best, and a net loss if there are more than 4 options. The moment you can eliminate even one wrong option, your real odds go up and the comparison shifts further in your favor.

Attempt Strategy: Fewer Questions vs. More Questions

A common mistake is assuming that attempting more questions always helps. Under negative marking, that's only true if your accuracy on the extra attempts stays above the break-even threshold for your exam. Below that threshold, every additional attempt you make is expected to reduce your score, not increase it.

Common trap: Attempting 10 extra questions where you're genuinely unsure (well below break-even confidence) to "not leave marks on the table" is one of the most common ways students lose score in negative-marking exams, even when their core knowledge is strong.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is negative marking calculated?
Final Score = (Correct Answers × Marks per Correct Answer) − (Wrong Answers × Marks per Correct Answer × Negative Ratio). Unattempted questions are not penalized in any major Indian competitive exam.
What is the negative marking ratio in NEET, JEE, and UPSC?
NEET and JEE Main award +4 for a correct answer and deduct 1 mark for a wrong answer (a 1/4 ratio). UPSC Prelims deducts one-third (1/3) of the allotted marks for a wrong answer. SSC CGL typically deducts one-fourth (1/4). Always confirm the exact ratio from the current official notification, since it can change between exam cycles.
Is negative marking applied to unattempted questions?
No. In NEET, JEE, UPSC, and SSC, only incorrectly answered questions attract a penalty. Leaving a question blank never reduces your score.
What is break-even probability in negative marking?
Break-even probability is the minimum confidence level (as a percentage of eliminated wrong options) at which guessing an answer stops being a net loss on average. It's calculated as Negative Ratio ÷ (1 + Negative Ratio). For a 1/4 ratio, this works out to 20% — meaning if you can eliminate at least 1 of 4 options, an educated guess is mathematically worth attempting.
Should I guess in an exam with negative marking?
It depends on the exam's negative ratio and the number of answer options — there's no single universal answer. Compare your real odds of being correct (blind, or after eliminating options) to the break-even threshold for your exam's ratio: for NEET/JEE's 1/4 ratio with 4 options, even a blind guess (25% odds) clears the 20% break-even bar, while UPSC's 1/3 ratio needs at least a 25%+ chance to break even. This calculator's Break-Even tool shows the exact threshold for any ratio so you can compare it to your own confidence.
Does attempting more questions always give a higher score?
No. Under negative marking, attempting more questions only helps if your accuracy on those extra attempts stays above the break-even threshold for that exam. Below that threshold, attempting more questions can lower your score compared to leaving them blank.
