PFT Calculator Guide: USMC Physical Fitness Test Scoring Explained

How pull-ups, push-ups, the plank, and the run or row turn into a 300-point score, and what changed in 2026

Looking for a different PFT?

This page covers the Marine Corps Physical Fitness Test: pull-ups or push-ups, a plank hold, and a 3-mile run or row. If you are looking for a Pulmonary Function Test, a medical breathing test read from a spirometer, this is not that calculator.

The Marine Corps Physical Fitness Test, or PFT, scores every Marine on three events: a pull-up or push-up set, a plank hold, and a 3-mile run, each worth up to 100 points for a maximum of 300. A PFT calculator turns your reps and times into that score without you having to look up eight separate age-group charts by hand.

This PFT calculator covers every age group from 17 to 51 and older, both sexes, the plank, the altitude-adjusted run, the 5,000 meter row alternative, and the January 2026 combat-arms standard. Enter your numbers above to see your score.

Quick answer:

A PFT score is the sum of three events, each worth up to 100 points (70 for push-ups), for a maximum of 300. You need at least 40 points on every event (60 for female pull-ups) and a 150-point total to pass. Scoring 235 or more earns 1st Class, 200 to 234 earns 2nd Class, and 150 to 199 earns 3rd Class.

1. How the PFT Calculator Works

Each event is scored against a table specific to your sex and age group, using eight age groups from 17-20 up to 51 and older. Recruits in initial recruit training are scored in the 17-20 group regardless of their actual age. Officer candidates are scored in the group that matches their age at the time of testing.

Pull-ups and push-ups are scored on separate tables, not one shared scale: the same rep count means a different score depending on which event you chose. Every table works the same way, a minimum rep count or time earns the minimum points, a maximum earns the cap, and everything between is scored on a straight line between those two points, rounded to the nearest whole point.

The plank is the only event scored on a single table for every Marine, regardless of age or sex. The run and row use their own age and sex tables, and the run has a separate table for testing at or above 4,500 feet, since thinner air makes the same effort slower.

Assumptions:

Falling short of the minimum on any single event fails that event, and failing one event fails the whole PFT regardless of your total. This calculator models each table as a straight line between its published minimum and maximum, which reproduced all 400+ official table cells it was compared against on October 18, 2026 (male 3-mile run, altitude run and 5,000m row rows, plus sample pull-up, push-up and plank rows), though it has not been checked against every cell of every table, including the female run and row tables; however, official results must come from an authorized test administrator and MCTIMS, not this or any unofficial calculator.

2. How to Use the PFT Calculator

  1. Choose your sex and enter your age, or check Recruit if you are in initial recruit training, which always uses the 17-20 age group.
  2. Turn on the Combat Arms 2026 standard if it applies to your military occupational specialty.
  3. Choose pull-ups or push-ups and enter your reps.
  4. Enter your plank hold time in minutes and seconds.
  5. Choose the 3-mile run or the 5,000 meter row, enter your time, and turn on the altitude toggle if you tested at or above 4,500 feet.
  6. Review your per-event points, total score, and class.

3. PFT Scoring Formula

Points (reps) = min_points + (your reps - min reps) / (max reps - min reps) x (max_points - min_points) Points (time) = 100 - (your time - best time) / (worst time - best time) x 60

Both formulas round to the nearest whole point, cap at the event's maximum, and score 0 if you fall short of the minimum. The official run and row tables list times in 10-second and 5-second steps, so a time between two rows scores at the slower row (an 18:01 run scores like 18:10). Min/max points are 40/100 for pull-ups, plank, and the run or row, 60/100 for female pull-ups, and 40/70 for push-ups.

Your total is the sum of your upper body, plank, and run or row points:

Total = Upper body points + Plank points + Run or Row points Class = 1st (235-300), 2nd (200-234), 3rd (150-199), otherwise Unqualified

4. Worked Example

A 23-year-old male Marine, age group 21-25, does 15 pull-ups, holds a 2:30 plank, and runs 3 miles in 24:00.

EventResultTable RangePoints
Pull-ups15 reps5 (40 pts) to 23 (100 pts)73
Plank2:30 (150 sec)1:10 (40 pts) to 3:45 (100 pts)71
3-Mile Run24:0018:00 (100 pts) to 27:40 (40 pts)63

Pull-ups: 40 + (15-5) ÷ (23-5) × 60 = 40 + 10⁄18 × 60 = 40 + 33.3 = 73.3, rounded to 73.

Plank: 150 seconds falls between the 70-second minimum and 225-second maximum: 40 + (150-70) ÷ (225-70) × 60 = 40 + 80⁄155 × 60 = 40 + 31 = 71.

Run: 24:00 (1,440 sec) falls between the 18:00 max (1,080 sec) and 27:40 min (1,660 sec): 100 - (1,440-1,080) ÷ (1,660-1,080) × 60 = 100 - 360⁄580 × 60 = 100 - 37.2 = 62.8, rounded to 63.

Total: 73 + 71 + 63 = 207 points, which is 2nd Class, since it falls between 200 and 234 and every event cleared its 40-point minimum.

5. What Changed in 2026

Effective January 1, 2026, Marines in combat arms occupational specialties, infantry, artillery, reconnaissance, and similar roles, are scored on the male, age-normed PFT tables regardless of their own sex, and must reach at least 210 points, 70 percent of the 300-point maximum, under MARADMIN 613/25, announced by the Marine Corps on December 18, 2025. General PFT scoring for every other Marine is unchanged.

A separate message, MARADMIN 066/26, replaced the twice-yearly height and weight check with a waist-to-height ratio standard of 0.52 or less. That change affects body composition screening, not the PFT score itself, so it is not part of this calculator.

6. Understanding Your Result

Your total score is what goes on record, but the class it falls into matters just as much: 1st Class (235-300), 2nd Class (200-234), and 3rd Class (150-199) all show up on fitness reports and factor into promotion boards and some special-duty eligibility. Below 150, or below the minimum on any single event, is Unqualified regardless of how high the other events scored.

If you selected the Combat Arms 2026 standard, the calculator also checks the 210-point floor separately from the class. A score can be 2nd Class by the general PFT scale and still fall short of, or clear, the combat-arms-specific minimum.

7. Pull-ups, Push-ups, Plank, Run and Row

Pull-ups and push-ups are genuinely different scales, not one event with two names. Twenty pull-ups is a maximum score for a 17-20 year-old man; the equivalent push-up count is worth far less, and push-ups can never exceed 70 points no matter how many you do. If you can do pull-ups at all, they are the higher-ceiling choice.

The plank replaced the abdominal crunch at the end of 2022 and uses one table for every Marine, so age and sex do not change the time you need to hold. The run and row both offer an age- and sex-normed path to 100 points, and Marines 46 or older, or with medical or postpartum approval, may row 5,000 meters instead of running.

Altitude genuinely changes the run: testing at 4,500 feet or higher adds 90 seconds to both the maximum and minimum time at every age group, reflecting the extra effort thinner air demands. This calculator applies that adjustment automatically when you turn on the altitude toggle.

8. Practical Tips

  • Do pull-ups if you can pass the minimum. The 30-point ceiling gap between pull-ups and push-ups is the single biggest lever on your total score.
  • Enter your actual test-day age group, not your age on some other date. The PFT is conducted between January and June each year, so your age at the actual test matters.
  • If your unit tests above 4,500 feet, use the altitude toggle. Skipping it will understate how well you actually performed.
  • Only Marines in initial recruit training should check the Recruit box. Officer candidates are scored by their actual age group, so they should enter their age instead.
  • A near-minimum score on every event adds up to well under 150. Build a real margin on at least one event rather than aiming exactly at the minimums.

9. Common Mistakes

  • Assuming pull-ups and push-ups share a scale: the same rep count scores differently on each table, and push-ups cap at 70 points regardless of reps.
  • Forgetting the per-event minimum: a strong total built on a failed event still does not pass the PFT.
  • Using the wrong age group: age groups run in 5-year bands except 51 and older, so a birthday near a test date can move you into a different table.
  • Ignoring altitude: the same run time is worth more points at altitude, since the altitude table's times are 90 seconds slower at both ends.
  • Confusing this with a medical PFT: a Pulmonary Function Test measures lung function with a spirometer and has nothing to do with reps or run times.

11. Sources and Methodology

Scoring tables and rules in this calculator come from Marine Corps Order 6100.13A and its scoring tables for pull-ups, push-ups, plank, run, and row, cross-checked across multiple independent transcriptions of those tables where the order itself was not directly reachable. You can compare any result against the official tables on the Marine Corps Fitness Test Standards page. The 3-mile run and altitude run tables were last checked against the official Table 2-4 and Table 2-5 on October 18, 2026; run times that fall between two table rows are scored at the slower row, as the official tables do. The January 2026 combat arms standard comes from the Marine Corps' own announcement of MARADMIN 613/25.

A PFT score comes down to three tables, one for your upper body event, one shared plank table, and one for your run or row, each converting your result into points on a straight scale between a published minimum and maximum.
