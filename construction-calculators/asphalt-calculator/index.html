Asphalt Calculator Guide: How Many Tons of Asphalt Do You Need?

Tonnage, volume, coverage and material cost for driveways and paving

An asphalt calculator turns the size of a paving job into the tons of hot mix asphalt (HMA) to order. Enter the length, width and compacted thickness, and this asphalt calculator returns weight in tons or tonnes, volume in cubic yards or cubic metres, and an optional material cost. It suits homeowners planning a driveway, contractors checking a bid, and anyone patching or overlaying a parking area. Plans and tape measures give you area and thickness, but asphalt is sold by weight, so figuring asphalt means bridging the two with density. A shortfall can stop a crew mid-job, and a surplus wastes money. Use the calculator above, then read on for the formula, coverage and cost.

Quick answer:

Tons = area (ft²) × thickness (in) × density (lb/ft³) ÷ 24,000. A 20 ft × 20 ft driveway paved 2.5 inches thick at 145 lb/ft³ needs about 6.04 tons, or about 6.34 tons with a 5% allowance.

How the Asphalt Calculator Works

The calculator finds the volume of the finished pavement layer by multiplying area by compacted thickness, adds your allowance, then multiplies by the mix density to get weight. It reports that weight as US tons and metric tonnes, along with volume in cubic yards, cubic feet and cubic metres. If you enter a price per ton, it also gives the material cost and the cost per square foot or square metre.

It assumes an even, flat layer. For an irregular shape, split it into rectangles and add the results, or enter the total area directly. The result covers the asphalt only, not the stone base beneath it, and it does not size millings, tack coat or sealcoat.

How to Use the Asphalt Calculator

  1. Choose US or metric units.
  2. Pick a preset (patch or overlay, driveway, heavy-duty) or enter your own thickness.
  3. Enter length and width, or switch to total area.
  4. Choose standard or dense mix density, or enter your supplier's figure under Custom.
  5. Set an allowance for waste and measurement error.
  6. Optionally enter your price per ton, then read the tonnage, volume and cost.

Asphalt Calculation Formula

Volume = Area × Compacted thickness Order volume = Volume × (1 + Allowance %) Weight = Order volume × Density Tons = Pounds ÷ 2,000

Keep area and thickness in consistent units before multiplying. The calculator works in metres internally and converts the result. The standard density is 145 lb/ft³ (about 2,323 kg/m³), and the dense option is about 150 lb/ft³ (2,400 kg/m³).

For a hand check in US units, tons = (square feet × thickness in inches × density in lb/ft³) ÷ 24,000, where 24,000 is 12 inches per foot times 2,000 lb per ton. Paving crews often use a shortcut instead: tons = square yards × thickness in inches × 110 ÷ 2,000, since one square yard of mix one inch thick weighs roughly 110 lb.

Worked Example

A 20 ft × 20 ft driveway paved 2.5 inches thick with standard mix at 145 lb/ft³ and a 5% allowance:

Cross-check with the field rule: 400 ft² is 44.4 yd², and 44.4 × 2.5 × 110 lb is about 12,222 lb, or 6.11 tons before allowance. That is close to the 6.04 tons from the main formula, because 110 lb per square yard per inch corresponds to a slightly higher density of about 147 lb/ft³.

How Far Does a Ton of Asphalt Go?

Coverage is the reverse question: how much area does a ton cover at a given thickness? At 145 lb/ft³, one US ton covers about 165 square feet at 1 inch, and coverage falls in proportion as thickness rises.

Asphalt Mix Types and Density

Asphalt, also called blacktop or tarmac, is aggregate bound with bitumen. Hot mix asphalt is the most common type and is the basis for the standard density here. Warm mix is made at lower temperatures, and driveway mixes are tailored for residential loads. Mixes are also named by size: a 12.5 mix has a nominal maximum aggregate size of 12.5 mm, about half an inch.

Density is the main variable. Pennsylvania Asphalt Pavement Association guidance puts typical compacted mix at roughly 108 to 120 lb per square yard per inch, or about 144 to 160 lb/ft³, which brackets the 145 and 150 lb/ft³ options above. Asphalt millings, recycled asphalt pavement, are sold by weight and vary by source, so use Custom with your supplier's density rather than the hot mix figure.

Asphalt Cost per Ton and per Square Foot

Material cost is simply tons multiplied by your supplier's price per ton, and the calculator also divides it by area to give a cost per square foot. At 2.5 inches and 145 lb/ft³, one square foot needs about 0.015 tons, so a hypothetical price of $100 per ton gives roughly $1.51 per square foot before allowance, or about $604 for a 400 ft² driveway. With a 5% allowance those become about $1.59 per square foot and $634, which are the figures the calculator shows. The $100 is an illustration only, so enter your own quote.

An asphalt driveway cost estimate or overlay quote from a contractor will be higher than this material figure, because it also covers base preparation, delivery, equipment, labor and sometimes sealing. Use the calculator to check that a quoted tonnage is reasonable, and ask what thickness and density the quote assumes.

Understanding Your Result

The main figure is what to order, allowance included. Suppliers sell by the ton, and plants load by truck, so round up to the next sensible load. The result is an estimate: real density, ground evenness and how the crew spreads the mix all move the final tonnage. This asphalt estimator uses finished thickness, not the thicker loose depth laid before rolling, so do not add compaction on top of your allowance.

Units and Conversions

  • 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet = 0.7646 cubic metres.
  • 1 US ton = 2,000 lb = 907 kg. 1 metric tonne = 1,000 kg.
  • At 145 lb/ft³, one cubic yard weighs about 3,915 lb, or 1.96 tons.
  • 1 square yard = 9 square feet = 0.836 square metres.

Practical Tips

  • Measure the length and width of the actual paved surface, and check the thickness your contractor specifies.
  • Ask your supplier for the mix density and whether the ton is a US ton or a tonne.
  • Keep a 5% allowance for a small, simple area and consider more for an irregular edge or uneven base.
  • For separate areas, calculate each and add the results.
  • Order close to the paving date, since hot mix must be placed soon after it is loaded.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing units: entering thickness in inches while the area is in square metres, or the reverse.
  • Using loose depth: thickness should be the finished, compacted depth.
  • Confusing tons and tonnes: a US ton is about 907 kg, a tonne is 1,000 kg.
  • Treating the cost as a final price: material cost excludes base work, labor and equipment.
  • Ignoring density: a different mix or millings can change the tonnage noticeably.
